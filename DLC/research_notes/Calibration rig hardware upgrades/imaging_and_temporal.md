## 1. Which scientific / machine-vision / astro / mirrorless cameras suit luminance mapping (bit depth, linearity, noise, DR, shutter, cooling, Python SDK, trigger, EU price)?

### Takeaway
A cooled **monochrome astro camera on a Sony IMX571 (APS-C, 16-bit, ~1–3.3 e⁻ read noise, ~14 stops/frame) or IMX533 (9 MP square, cheaper)** gives the best spatial-luminance value: no demosaic/ISP, near-zero dark current, exposures from µs to minutes (so 5+ decades is reachable with 2–3 bracketed frames + binning), and a usable Python path (`zwoasi`). Machine-vision global-shutter cameras (Basler/FLIR/Daheng, €218–1,119) are cheaper per unit and better for **triggered/temporal** work but have less per-frame DR and no cooling. A used full-frame mirrorless (e.g. Sony a7 III) gives ~14 bits of engineering DR in uncompressed RAW but is Bayer, uncooled and awkward to script. **No camera fixes veiling glare** — that is an optics/masking problem (Section 2) and is the real limit for halo-on-black.

### Inferences
- **Photon budget (my arithmetic, approximate):** sensor illuminance ≈ π·L/(4N²). At L = 1,800 cd/m², f/8 → ≈22 lx; a 3.76 µm pixel then collects ~1×10⁶ e⁻/s (assuming ~555 nm-equivalent photons, QE ≈ 0.9; a broadband white gives up to ~2× more electrons per lumen). 73 ke⁻ FW therefore saturates in roughly 30–70 ms on the 1,800-nit object. A 0.01-nit background at the same settings yields only ~5 e⁻/s/px → ~100 e⁻ needs ~15–20 s unbinned or ~1 s at 4×4 bin; dark current (0.002 e⁻/s at 0 °C) is negligible. **So 5+ decades is achievable with 2–3 bracketed exposures + binning on a cooled IMX571/IMX533; the sensor is not the bottleneck — veiling glare is (Section 2).**
- **Single-frame DR:** 73 ke⁻ / 3.3 e⁻ ≈ 22,000:1 ≈ 4.3 decades (ZWO's "14 stops"); 50 ke⁻ or 44 ke⁻ FW lowers this to ~4.1 decades. Not enough for 5 decades in one shot; bracket.
- **Rolling shutter is fine for static halo maps** if every exposure is an integer multiple of the refresh period (16.667 ms at 60 Hz) and ≥ ~6 refreshes long, so each row integrates the same number of whole frames/PWM cycles; this also suggests an ND (~0.3–1.0) for the brightest highlights so exposures stay ≥100 ms without saturating.
- **Sensor format vs. the 32" 16:9 screen (708×399 mm):** APS-C 23.5×15.7 mm (IMX571, 3:2) frames the screen with little waste; the square IMX533 uses only ~50 % of its rows on a 16:9 screen (≈3008×1580 effective px) — still ~4.7 MP over the panel. IMX492 (19.2×13.1 mm, computed from 4144×4.63 µm) sits between.
- **Mono beats Bayer for this job** (no demosaic, no ISP, no CFA-induced spatial interpolation), and cooling makes long dim-background exposures clean. Bayer mirrorless (a7 III) is a viable *cheaper* option if a used body is to hand, but scripting/tethering on Windows is less clean than `zwoasi`/`pypylon` (not verified here).
- **IMX571 vs IMX533:** IMX571 buys ~3× the pixels and a better aspect fit for ~€1,000 more; for halo maps (mm-scale structures) the IMX533's ~0.24 mm/px over the screen is already ample. IMX533 is the value pick; IMX571 is the "one camera for everything" pick.
- Global-shutter astro (ASI432MM, 9 µm, 97 ke⁻, 12-bit, 120 fps) is an interesting **hybrid**: big pixels/high FW for single-frame DR (~97k/2.9 ≈ 33,000:1 ≈ 4.5 decades, if both specs hold at the same gain — unverified) plus global shutter for temporal ROI work, uncooled.
- LUCID IMX490 on-sensor 120 dB HDR is attractive on paper (6 decades single-shot) but it is colour-only and its HDR merge is an on-sensor multi-exposure/split-pixel scheme whose linearity at the merge points would itself need characterising — not a clean photometric path.

## 2. What optics (lens, working distance, hoods/baffles, ND, V(λ)/RGB filters) and veiling-glare suppression does a 32" screen need?

### Takeaway
Use a **short-to-normal prime stopped down to ~f/5.6–8 at 1.1–1.7 m** (keeps screen-edge view angles ≤ ~17°), a deep flocked hood, and — the decisive part — **physical occlusion of the bright object (flat mask/frustum, the "masking method")** rather than post-hoc deconvolution. Literature shows lens/camera glare caps uncorrected scene contrast at only ~500–600:1 in typical HDR scenes and that occlusion masks lift it to ~10⁴:1; a V(λ) glass filter costs ~£52–118, flocking ~£36–50 per 60 in.

### Cited Findings
- **Veiling glare is the limit of HDR capture:** bright objects reduce contrast everywhere in the field via multiple scattering in lens, body and sensor; Talvala et al. (SIGGRAPH 2007) compare deconvolution by a measured glare spread function vs. direct/indirect separation with a translated **structured occlusion mask**, the mask giving "significantly higher" SNR — [Stanford project page](https://graphics.stanford.edu/papers/glare_removal/)
- Cozzi et al. 2018 (J. Imaging 4(8):100) reuse the occlusion mask; reported contrast ratios rose from 560:1 → 22,400:1 and 505:1 → 8,850:1 for two test objects and 603:1 → 1,503:1 for a whole scene; post-capture deconvolution amplifies noise in glare-heavy regions — [MDPI J. Imaging](https://www.mdpi.com/2313-433X/4/8/100/htm)
- Small dark targets on bright fields suffer large veiling-glare errors; NIST describes a **replica-mask** method + a diagnostic; a **gloss-black frustum** (truncated cone) reduces veiling glare better than matte black (it traps light by multiple reflections); a flat mask only beats the frustum if it is in contact with the screen and the pixel-to-front-surface distance is ~1 mm — and touching an LCD can alter it; NISTIR 6637 covers making seamless plastic frusta — [NIST display-metrology summaries (search snippets)](https://www.nist.gov/node/684751); [NISTIR 6637](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/IR/nistir6637.pdf)
- Stray light inside imaging luminance meters is the **main error source for halo measurement** (Everfine, SID Display Week 2025); their improved method was validated against the traditional **masking method** — [SID Digest 37-4](https://sid.org/Portals/sid/Files/SID-Digest/docs/37-4.pdf)
- A 2D imaging luminance meter for LED displays: stray light caused ~7 % luminance overestimation, reduced to ~2 % by the authors' correction (arXiv, Dec 2025) — [arXiv 2512.23051](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23051v1)
- A 2025 CIE-proceedings paper reportedly found larger apertures (small f-number) increase diffuse stray light around bright sources (search-snippet attribution; not read in full) — [HAL / LNE 2025 proceedings](https://hal.umontpellier.fr/LNE/hal-05554049v1)
- PTB's HiDyn project: camera-internal stray light must be characterised and uncertainty budgets need better evaluation of it — [PTB HiDyn summary](https://www.hidyn.ptb.de/fileadmin/epm2021/hidyn/documents/21NRM01_M1_Publishable_Summary__APPROVED_.pdf)
- Imatest sells a veiling-glare mask + frame ($140–180); method = image with/without mask at identical exposure — [Imatest store](https://store.imatest.com/imatest-veiling-glare-mask-plus-frame.html)
- TestUFO warns that lens smudges/fingerprints create halo-like artefacts not caused by the display — [TestUFO blooming test](https://www.testufo.com/blooming)
- ICDM **IDMS v1.3 (2025)** adds extensive material on setting up cameras/imaging light-measuring devices, including **moiré-reduction techniques**; the PDF is a free download — [SID/ICDM standards](https://www.sid.org/Standards/ICDM)
- **Photopic (V(λ)) glass**, UQG Optics FPE series (unit price, VAT basis inconsistent across page versions): 12.5 mm £51.80 (AR-coated £103), 25 mm £58.50, 25×25 mm £62, 50 mm £115, 50×50 mm £118 — [UQG photopic filters](https://www.uqgoptics.com/product/photopic-filter-2/); Midwest Optical BP530-52 = M52×0.75-threaded photopic bandpass for mono cameras (price not shown) — [Midwest Optical](https://marketing.dev.machinevisiondirect.com/products/filters/product/BP530-52); Comar "540 GB" laminated photopic filter mountable in camera filter rings — [Comar Optics](https://www.comaroptics.com/components/filters/glass-colour-filters)

### Inferences
- **Geometry (computed):** 32" 16:9 = 708×399 mm. Framing ~760 mm wide: APS-C (23.5 mm) → m ≈ 0.031 → object distance ≈ 33×f: 35 mm → ~1.17 m, 50 mm → ~1.67 m, 85 mm → ~2.8 m. IMX533 (11.3 mm) → m ≈ 0.015 → ≈68×f: 16 mm → ~1.1 m, 25 mm → ~1.7 m. Edge/corner view angles: at 1.67 m ≈ 12°/14°; at 1.17 m ≈ 17°/19°; at a phone-like 0.5 m ≈ 35°/39°. **Much of the S25's 1.0→0.2 centre-to-edge falloff is plausibly lens vignetting + panel angular falloff at short distance**; going to ≥1.2 m with a longer lens shrinks both. Telecentric optics are not needed (and would have to be ≥ 800 mm aperture).
- **Moiré:** 3840 px over 708 mm = 0.184 mm display pitch; IMX571 over 760 mm = 0.122 mm/px (~1.5 camera px per display px) → moiré-prone. Bin ≥ 4×4, slightly defocus, or follow IDMS v1.3's moiré guidance; halo modelling does not need sub-display-pixel sampling.
- **Lens choice:** a fixed-focal prime with few elements, good multi-coating, stopped to f/5.6–8 (stray light rises at wide apertures per the CIE-paper claim); clean front element; deep hood lined with flocking; put the V(λ)/RGB filter **behind** the lens (filter wheel at the sensor, slightly tilted) to avoid a flat front filter creating ghost reflections of the bright object. Machine-vision C-mount 16/25 mm lenses suit IMX533/IMX287; any 35–50 mm photo prime with an adapter suits APS-C. (Prices not researched.)
- **Masking recipe for FALD halos (the high-value cheap step, works even with the S25):** the halo originates in the backlight *behind* the LC layer, so a gloss-black or flocked opaque mask held a few mm in front of the bright object (not touching the panel) blocks the object's direct light from the camera while leaving the halo region visible → camera glare from the object is removed at the source. Capture (a) object+halo unmasked, (b) masked, (c) black frame; difference (a)−(b) near the mask edge estimates the camera's own glare spread function for later deconvolution of unmaskable scenes. A cone/frustum on a single photodiode or spot meter gives a glare-free point reference for cross-checking the map.
- **ND:** not needed for exposure range on a cooled astro camera (µs-to-minutes exposures); useful only to keep highlight exposures ≥ ~6 refresh periods (see Section 1).
- **V(λ) filter vs RGB filter wheel:** for an additive 3-primary display, a mono camera + 3 broad R/G/B filters, each calibrated against the i1Display Pro on full-field R, G, B patches, can reconstruct per-pixel Y (and approximate XYZ) exactly to the extent the primaries' spectra are stable — no V(λ) match needed; a V(λ) filter alone gives Y in one shot but its f1′ mismatch error grows for narrow-band (QD/KSF-type) primaries and for LC-leakage light whose spectrum differs from the white the camera was calibrated on. Recommend: single V(λ) filter for fast Y maps, RGB filters for leakage-colour checks.

## 3. Imaging colorimeters / luminance cameras: what do entry-level units cost, are used ones < €5k realistic, and what is the best DIY approximation?

### Takeaway
Every commercial imaging photometer/colorimeter found (Konica Minolta CA-2500, Radiant ProMetric I/I16-G, Admesy Atlas/Helios, TechnoTeam LMK, Everfine CX) is **quote-only**; no public or used prices were found, so a used unit < €5k could not be confirmed as realistic. They also suffer the same internal stray-light problem for halos (Everfine). The best DIY approximation is **cooled mono camera + V(λ) or RGB filters + flat-field + HDR bracketing + i1Display Pro cross-calibration + masking for glare**, which literature shows reaches ~6 % absolute luminance error even with low-cost hardware.

With a 1 MΩ TIA, 0.01 nit ≈ 1.4 mV — measurable with averaging. The 2856 K calibration illuminant ≠ display spectrum, so cross-calibrate to the i1D3 per primary.

Add a second diode on a DesktopLUT-drawn sync square to timestamp frames, and a third on a neighbouring zone for zone-to-zone timing.
- OSRTT is useful as a cheap cross-check of LC response times on the BenQ/ProArt in SDR, but its ~160-nit target and lack of strobing filtering make it unsuitable for HDR FALD timing.

## 5. High-speed video: affordable high-fps cameras vs a photodiode for FALD timing

## 6. What do display reviewers and academic mini-LED halo papers actually use for halo/blooming and backlight-timing measurements?

### Cited Findings

## 7. Which options rank best by value for THIS rig (cheap → stretch), and how would each be used?

### Inferences (ranking and usage plan)
1. **Glare control kit (≤ €150; do first):** flocked hood/tube, a set of gloss-black/flocked masks (and one frustum for spot checks), UQG 25/50 mm V(λ) glass. Use with the S25 immediately: masked vs unmasked captures separate panel halo from lens glare (the +3–4 % dim-bar contamination). Cheapest way to un-park the halo model's data quality problem.
2. **Temporal chain (≈ €450–700):** S9219/S9219-01 or PDA100A2(+photopic glass) → AD3 (Python-native, 14-bit, streams ~10 MS/s; best fit for DLC scripting) or DHO804 (4 ch, 25 Mpts, 12-bit; best as a bench scope). Measures zone rise/fall, per-refresh ripple, PWM frequency/depth, motion flashes at points; 2nd/3rd channel = sync square + neighbouring zone. Highest information-per-euro for the temporal questions; also supports flicker-percentage/SVM-style metrics (≥ 5 kS/s, ≥ 1 s).
3. **Cooled mono camera (≈ €1,000 IMX533 → ≈ €1,500–2,600 IMX571; IMX571 is STRETCH at EU street prices):** + 25 mm (IMX533) or 35–50 mm (APS-C) prime at f/5.6–8, 1.1–1.7 m; RGB/V(λ) filter wheel; bias/dark library at fixed TEC setpoint; refresh-multiple bracketed exposures; per-primary i1D3 cross-cal; flat-field; masking. Delivers the "dense camera maps" the parked FALD model is waiting for. Over the phone it buys: true linear mono RAW without ISP/denoise, 16-bit (IMX571), µs–minutes exposures, cooling, scriptable Python control, rigid geometry.
4. **Global-shutter 500 fps MV camera (≈ €218–449):** IMX287 for 2 ms spatial-temporal ROI maps (zone ramps, parity stagger, motion flashes) or the a2A1920-160umPRO for a general triggered 1920×1200 camera with `pypylon`. Pairs with (2) for the equivalent-time trick.
5. **STRETCH, low marginal value here:** QHY268M/ASI2600 full LRGB kits (> €2,500) buy convenience, not new capability over (3); Chronos-class high-speed (≈ $5k, 2021) adds sub-ms full-frame video the stated needs do not require; commercial imaging colorimeters (quote-only, likely ≫ €5k) buy traceable XYZ maps but still need masking for halos.
- **Used market:** eBay.de showed a used ASI2600MM Pro at €2,200 (private sale, no warranty) — the replacement of the Pro by the P25 (Sept 2025) may push used Pro prices down; astro classifieds (astronomie.de) were not checked.
